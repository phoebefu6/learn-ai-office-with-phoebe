# Learn AI Office with Phoebe

Six 45-minute sessions, single track, and **one real weekly report** you build across all of them.

This is the honest version of the course. You have been told this technology makes you forty
percent faster, and on one task in a lab that is true. Across a measured working week it comes out
nearer two percent. Nobody lied to you, and understanding exactly why is worth more than any
prompt library.

**Live:** https://phoebefu6.github.io/learn-ai-office-with-phoebe/

## The sessions

| # | Session | What your report gains |
|---|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | The hour back | An honest baseline, and the four-part ask that earns most of the time |
| 2 | Documents | The written part drafted without losing the exception that matters |
| 3 | Spreadsheets | The numbers, with working you can actually check |
| 4 | Inbox and meetings | The inputs, without costing you trust |
| 5 | The report | The whole thing assembled, every figure traceable |
| 6 | Make it repeatable | Saved, habitual, and honest about what you never automate |

## The simulator

`assets/office-live.js` runs the same levers in two modes, and the gap between them is the course.

**One task** - the weekly report, which is what a lab experiment measures:

| Rung | Minutes | Faster by |
|------|---------|-----------|
| a bare ask | 170 | 8% |
| + attach the data and name the reader | 150 | 19% |
| + ask for a slice, not the whole document | 137 | 26% |
| + make it show its working | 128 | 31% |
| + check the figures before it goes | 122 | 34% |
| + a saved prompt you reuse | **111** | **40%** |

**Your whole week** - the same levers, with a days-per-week dial:

| Days you reach for it | Saved per week | As % of working hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27 min | 1.1% |
| 5 | 136 min | 5.7% |

Published surveys find 5.4% of hours among users and 1.4% across all workers. The simulator lands
on both ends of that range. Only 340 minutes of a 2,400-minute week are work this can touch at
all, and that is the first surprise.

One lever is a **null anti-lever**: "expect it to give you fewer meetings" is switchable, never
moves the number, and prints the measurement that proves it. Microsoft's own randomized trial of
56 firms and 6,000+ workers put meeting time at +2.38 minutes per week, not statistically
significant, while email reading fell 12.3 minutes.

## Honesty

Every number is labelled as vendor-reported or independent, with its sample size. Where a
much-quoted figure comes from a superseded paper version or a press release rather than an article
body, the pages say so. The full source map, with every URL and a dated "do not state as fact"
list, is in [`materials/official-course-map.md`](materials/official-course-map.md).
