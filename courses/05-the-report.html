Part 1 · covers the order you assemble in

Numbers first, and why the other order is expensive

6 min live

You have four sets of parts sitting in four different places. There is exactly one order to put them together in, and the wrong order does not merely waste time. It changes what the report says.

🎫 Clear all three quiz questions to stamp your passport Live = presented in the 45 minutes Self-study = the full depth, after class

★ What you walk out with tonight. One assembled report, a traceability table you can reuse every week, and the three-part check, including the omission check that almost nobody runs. On the task ladder this session is the step from 128 minutes to 122, which is 31 percent to 34 percent.

LiveWhy writing the prose first is the expensive mistake5 min▶

Almost everyone assembles in the wrong order, and it is an understandable one. Prose is the part that feels like the report, so you start there, and the drafting tool is very good at producing it, so you get something that looks finished within two minutes.

Then the numbers arrive and they do not quite match the sentence you wrote. And here is the trap, because you have a choice at that moment and it does not feel like a choice: soften the sentence, or find a number that supports it. Both are easier than rewriting a paragraph you already like.

You end up defending a sentence rather than reporting a number. That is the whole cost, and it is invisible from the outside because the finished document reads perfectly well.

Numbers first means a wrong sentence is cheap. You have not committed to a story yet, so when the figure comes back different from what you expected, changing the prose costs you nothing and you do it without noticing. That is the entire argument for the order.

Part 2 · covers traceability as a discipline

Every figure points at something you can open

6 min live

This is the rule that makes the difference between a report that survives a question and one that does not. It is one sentence long and it is completely unforgiving.

LiveThe traceability table, four columns, reused every week4 min▶

Keep it next to the report, not inside it. Four columns and no more, because a table nobody fills in is worse than no table.

FigureSourceDateOwner
Revenue, 1.42mfinance-aug.xlsx, cell D482026-08-24Finance

Hiring pausedops sync notes, decisions2026-08-21You

Three things this buys you. When someone questions a number in the room, you open the source rather than promising to check. When a figure changes next month, you know which of them to refresh. And when you eventually hand this report to someone else, the table is the handover.

The date column is the one people leave out and it is the one that earns its keep. A figure that was true three weeks ago and has been carried forward unchanged is the single most common way a report goes quietly wrong.

Self-studyThe mechanism behind a number that drifted4 min▶

When a figure comes out of a summarisation step slightly different from the source document, people usually assume a bug. It is not. Microsoft describes the behaviour in its own documentation, and it is worth quoting both halves.

First, Copilot "cannot understand meaning or evaluate accuracy". It is not checking your number against reality, because there is nothing in the process that could.

Second, and this is the mechanism: it is "optimized for summarization, transformation, and reasoning over content, not for guaranteed verbatim reproduction of arbitrarily large inputs".

Read that as a design statement rather than a limitation. Verbatim reproduction is not what the system is for. A number passing through a summarisation step is being transformed, not copied, and transformation of a number that nobody is checking is exactly how a figure drifts between a source document and a report.

Real world

This is also why the session 3 lever exists. "Show me the formula or the source line" makes the transformation visible. Traceability makes it checkable. Neither prevents the drift, and nothing you can buy does. They both make it survivable.

Thirty seconds, no model involved, and you now know the number rather than trusting it. Two of those per report is the whole recompute step, and it is the first part of the check.

Part 3 · covers the verify lever, 31 to 34 percent

The check that pays for itself

5 min live

This session owns one lever on the ladder and this is it. Three points on the task, which sounds small, and it is worth far more than three points because of what it prevents.

LiveThe check is three things, and none of them is re-reading5 min▶

Re-reading your own report does almost nothing, and everybody knows this in their bones while continuing to do it. You wrote it, so you read what you meant rather than what is on the page. The check has to be mechanical instead.

  • Recompute two figures. Not all of them. Two, picked because they are the ones a reader would react to. Open the source, redo the arithmetic yourself, compare. Ninety seconds each.
  • Confirm the exception is present. The thing you know matters, the one your boss will ask about, the one session 2 warned you disappears. Look for it by name.
  • Confirm nothing from last month has silently vanished. Put last month's report next to this one and scan the headings. Anything that was there and is not here now is either deliberate or an omission, and you need to know which.

That third one is the omission check, and almost nobody does it. It is the only step in this entire course that looks for something that is not there. Everything else in a review process looks at what is on the page, which is precisely the failure mode these tools do not have.

Total cost, on a real report: about six minutes. That is the six minutes between 128 and 122, and the simulator prices it exactly that way.

Numbers. Get the figures out first, with the working visible. Session 3 gave you defensible ones. Nothing else starts until these exist, because everything downstream describes them.

Frame. Now write the prose around the numbers you actually have. Section by section, not the whole document, because the middle of a long ask gets the least attention.

Inputs. Fold in the meeting decision and the inbox input from session 4. These are usually one sentence each and they are usually the part a reader cares about most.

Check. Recompute two figures. Confirm the exception is present. Confirm nothing from last month vanished. Six minutes, and this is the rung from 31 to 34 percent.

Send. A person reads it and a person sends it. Session 4's rule holds here too: nothing in this pipeline sends itself.

Part 4 · covers what happens without the check

What an unchecked report costs, handled carefully

3 min live

Short part, and I want to be precise about what is known here, because this story gets retold badly.

LiveThe episode, stated as it was reported3 min▶

As reported in the news: a large consultancy delivered a report to a government client that contained fabricated references and a misquoted judgment. The firm acknowledged the use of generative AI in producing it, issued a corrected version, and gave a partial refund.

Two things this course will not do with that story. It will not state the refund amount, because the reported figures vary and this page only carries numbers it can trace. And it will not call it a regulatory action, because it was not one. It was a commercial refund between a supplier and a client. Retelling it as a fine makes it a better story and a false one.

What it is genuinely useful for is the shape of the failure. Nobody shipped a document that looked wrong. It looked excellent, which is the whole problem, and the specific things that were wrong were references and a quotation. Both are exactly the kind of item a traceability table catches and a read-through does not, because a read-through checks whether the sentence reads well.

Your weekly report is not a government report. The mechanism is identical and it is much cheaper to fix at your scale, which is the only reason this is worth three minutes of a Tuesday evening.

Build-along

Assemble it end to end, then check it

★ 22 min · everyone builds

This is the session where the artifact becomes real. By the end of the next twenty-two minutes you have one finished weekly report, a traceability table beside it, and a grade you gave yourself honestly.

Open all four parts side by side. Numbers from session 3, frame from session 2, meeting and inbox inputs from session 4, and last month's report. That last one is not optional tonight.

Place the numbers first, with nothing around them. Bare figures in the order they will appear. Resist writing a single sentence until they are all down. This feels wrong and it is the point.

Now ask for the frame, section by section. Use the four-part ask, attach the figures, name the reader, give the shape, and add the honesty clause: if a number is missing, say so and do not estimate.

Fold in the meeting decision and the inbox input. One sentence each, in your own words if either is relational. Session 4's rule does not stop applying because you are inside a document.

Build the traceability table. One row per figure: figure, source, date, owner. Do it now, while you still remember where each number came from. Next week it takes two minutes because most rows carry forward.

Delete or flag every figure without a row. This is the uncomfortable step and usually there are one or two. Either find the source or write "source not confirmed" next to it in the report itself.

Run the check. Recompute two figures. Pick the two a reader would react to. Open the source, do the arithmetic yourself, compare.

Confirm the exception is present, by name. Search the document for it. Not "does the report feel complete", but "is this specific thing in it".

Run the omission check against last month. Scan last month's headings against this month's. Anything that has disappeared is either a decision you made or one made for you. Find out which.

Grade it below before you send it. Five rows, honestly. The score is for you and nobody else sees it.

Every figure traces to a source you can open in one click Not "it looked right" and not "I know where that came from". You can point at the cell, the line or the page right now.
The exception you know matters is present The thing your reader will ask about. You checked for it by name rather than reading for a general sense of completeness.
Nothing from last month vanished silently You put the two reports side by side and compared headings. Anything missing is missing on purpose and you can say why.
The ask is written down and reusable The four-part ask that produced the frame exists as text you saved, not as something you retyped from memory this week.
A person read it before it went End to end, by you, after assembly and after the check. Nothing in this pipeline sends itself, and that includes the final send.

If you take one thing from tonight. Put last month's report next to this month's and compare the headings. It takes ninety seconds, it is the only check anyone runs that looks for absence, and it is the one that catches the failure these tools actually have.

Homework

Before session 6

30 min
  1. Send the report. The real one, assembled tonight. This course has been building towards a document that actually goes out, and it does not count until it does.
  2. Keep the traceability table. Same file, next week. Note which rows carried forward unchanged, because that number tells you how much of next week is already done.
  3. Time the check honestly. How long did recompute plus exception plus omission actually take? Bring the number. Everyone guesses higher than the truth.
  4. Save the ask that produced the frame. Exactly as typed, in a file you can find. Session 6 is built on this and it will be embarrassing to arrive without it.
  5. Write down one thing you will never automate. There is at least one part of this report that must stay yours, and you now know your report well enough to name it. Session 6 opens with that list.

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Why does the assembly order start with numbers rather than prose?

Prose feels like the report and the tool produces it in two minutes, so almost everyone starts there. Then the figures disagree with the sentence and softening the sentence is easier than rewriting the paragraph. Numbers first makes a wrong sentence cheap, which is the entire argument.

2 · Which part of the three-part check is the one almost nobody runs?

It is the only step in the entire course that looks for something that is not there. Every other review habit examines what is on the page, which is exactly the failure mode these tools do not have. Omission does not look wrong, so it has to be checked for deliberately, against last month.

3 · How should the consultancy episode be described accurately?
