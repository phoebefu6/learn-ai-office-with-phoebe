## THE SPINE: the lab number is not your week

This is the course's argument, and every number in it is sourced.

**What controlled experiments find (single tasks):**
- Noy and Zhang, 444 college-educated professionals, preregistered writing tasks: time taken
  **-0.8 SD**, quality **+0.4 SD**. Also "ChatGPT mostly substitutes for worker effort rather
  than complementing worker skills". https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1.pdf
  Published in Science: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586
- Dell'Acqua et al., 758 BCG consultants, preregistered: **+12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster,
  40%+ higher quality**. And the honesty number from the same abstract: on a task chosen to sit
  outside the tool's frontier, consultants using AI were **19 percentage points less likely** to
  get it right. https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf
- Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, QJE 2025, 5,172 support agents: **+15%** issues resolved per
  hour, largest gains for the least experienced, "small speed gains and small quality declines"
  for the most experienced. https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658

**What measured whole workweeks find (an order of magnitude smaller):**
- **Microsoft's own randomized trial** - 56 firms, over 6,000 workers, 6 months, objective
  telemetry rather than self-report. Email reading **-12.30 min/week** (SE 1.23) intent-to-treat,
  **-31.16 min/week** among regular users. Document completion -0.477 days ITT. Adoption: "nearly
  40% of workers who were given access used it regularly."
  **The null nobody quotes: total meeting duration +2.38 min/week (SE 1.32), not statistically
  significant.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11443
- Microsoft Research telemetry, July 2024: "read 11% fewer individual emails and spent 4% less
  time interacting with them". On meetings, of 47 organizations **10 saw a significant decrease
  and 14 saw a significant increase**. Microsoft's own disclosure on the same page: "all work
  here was funded by Microsoft, which has a commercial interest in ... demonstrating the degree
  to which Copilot increases worker productivity."
  https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Generative-AI-in-Real-World-Workplaces.pdf
- Bick, Blandin and Deming, nationally representative US: "time savings equivalent to **1.4
  percent of total work hours**" across all workers, **5.4%** among users, which "implies a time
  savings of 2.2 hours per week". Their own caveat: workers "may take their time savings as
  on-the-job leisure, which would increase welfare but not productivity."
  https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32966/w32966.pdf
- Humlum and Vestergaard, Denmark, 25,000 workers across 7,000 workplaces. **Cite the current
  March 2026 revision**, retitled "Still Waters, Rapid Currents": "we estimate precise null
  effects on earnings and recorded hours ... **ruling out effects larger than 2%** two years
  after the launch of ChatGPT", and **85%** of users "report reallocating time savings from AI
  chatbots to other job tasks" while fewer than 10% take extra breaks.
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777

**The honest headline the sources support, and the course's thesis:** lab experiments on single
tasks show 25 to 40 percent speedups. Measured effects on real workweeks are an order of
magnitude smaller. **The gap is dilution, not deception** - people use the tool every third or
fourth day, and the saved time gets reallocated to other work rather than banked.

## Honesty rails carried on the pages

1. **The dilution gap is the point.** Never quote a lab speedup without the measured-week number
   next to it. The simulator makes this mechanical.
2. **Meetings did not go down.** Microsoft's own RCT found no significant change, and its
   telemetry study found more organizations where meetings went UP than down. Anyone promising
   fewer meetings is selling.
3. **Omission beats hallucination as the real failure mode.** The Gavel study, 12 frontier
   models, 100 long cases, 160 hours of human annotation: "current models are more prone to
   omitting key information than hallucinating ... The main failure mode is omission rather than
   hallucination." Best model scored F1 59.1 with the checklist in the prompt.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04424 Microsoft's own beginning-and-end prioritisation note is the
   mechanism.
4. **Spreadsheet accuracy has a number.** SpreadsheetBench: human experts **71.33%** overall.
   Microsoft reports Agent Mode in Excel at **57.2%** on the full task set. Say out loud that the
   two figures come from two different evaluators.
   https://spreadsheetbench.github.io/ · https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14991v2
5. **Drafted email costs trust, and there is a study.** Cardon and Coman, 1,100 working
   professionals: "managers who use AI for routine communication tasks put their trustworthiness
   at risk when using medium- to high-levels of AI assistance", with respondents questioning
   "authorship, confidence, caring, sincerity, and ability".
   https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23294884251350599 The exact percentages come from a
   university press release, not the article body, so cite direction not decimals.
6. **Oversharing is the risk nobody expects.** Microsoft's own words: "generative AI amplifies
   the problem of oversharing data." Neither product breaks permissions. Both make a decade of
   sloppy permissions instantly searchable in plain language.
   https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/data-security-posture-management-oversharing
7. **Prompt injection arrives in ordinary office files.** Anthropic, on Word: injections hide "in
   document content such as text, comments, tracked changes, headers, and footers". Microsoft has
   XPIA classifiers but says "These classifiers may not be available in all Microsoft Copilot
   scenarios."
8. **The EU carve-out nobody quotes:** "Models provided by Anthropic as a subprocessor are
   currently excluded from the EU Data Boundary."
   https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy

## Simulator canon - VERIFIED IN-BROWSER 2026-08-24

`assets/office-live.js`. Two modes over one set of levers. Every number below was read out of the
live simulator after tuning, not estimated. Session pages must quote these exactly.

**Model constants:** a 40-hour week is 2,400 minutes. Only **340 of those minutes** are work this
can touch at all - that is 14 percent, and the surprise in that number is itself a teaching point.
The weekly report task baseline is 185 minutes.

**TASK mode** - one weekly report, which is what a lab experiment measures:

| Rung | Minutes | Faster by | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|
| a bare ask | 170 | 8% | session 1 |
| + attach the data and name the reader | 150 | 19% | session 1 |
| + ask for a slice, not the whole document | 137 | 26% | session 2 |
| + make it show its working | 128 | 31% | session 3 |
| + check the figures before it goes | 122 | 34% | session 5 |
| + a saved prompt you reuse | **111** | **40%** | session 6 |

40 percent on one task sits at the top of the published single-task band (BCG consultants 25.1%
faster, support agents +15%). This is the number people quote.

**WEEK mode** - the same levers across a real week, with the days-per-week dial:

| Days you reach for it | Saved per week | As % of working hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27 min | **1.1%** |
| 2 | 54 min | 2.3% |
| 3 | 82 min | 3.4% |
| 4 | 109 min | 4.5% |
| 5 | 136 min | **5.7%** |

Against the measured studies: **5.4%** of hours among users and **1.4%** across all workers
(Bick, Blandin and Deming), and 2.2 hours per week among users. The simulator lands on both ends
of that range without being fitted to it.

**THE GAP, which is the whole course:** 40 percent on one task, 2.3 percent of your week at two
days a week. Nothing about the tool changed between those two numbers. Only how much of the week
it touches, and how often you reach for it.

**The locked top rung is real.** Without the session-6 saved-prompt lever the ceiling is 34% on
the task and 4.8% on the week. Gated behind 4 passport stamps with an honest bypass.

**The two traps:**

| Trap | Effect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| skip the check and send it out | 5.7% falls to **4.4%** | rework costs more than review, and the failure is usually an omission |
| expect it to give you fewer meetings | **moves the number by exactly 0.0** | Microsoft's own RCT: meeting time +2.38 min/week, NOT significant. Telemetry study: 10 orgs down, **14 orgs up** |

The meetings lever is a **null anti-lever** - it is switchable, it never helps, and switching it on
prints the measurement that proves it. Reusable pattern for any "everyone believes this and it is
not true" lesson.

**What is real vs simulated:** the reference bars are real published figures with their sample
sizes on the page. The lever weights are a teaching model calibrated so that the endpoints land on
the measured studies - they are not a measurement of your job.
